Emergency water damage in Uniondale, NY: what to do first

If you searched for water damage restoration Uniondale NY because water is spreading across a floor right now, start with the source. Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the water, removing it, drying every wet material and documenting the result so the building is safe to rebuild. Uniondale Water Damage Pros runs a 24/7 emergency dispatch line for homes and businesses in Uniondale, NY and nearby parts of Nassau County.

While you wait for a crew, shut off the main water valve if the source is a pipe or fixture. Stay away from any outlet, cord or appliance that touches wet floor. If the panel is dry and you can reach it safely, turn off power to the wet area. If you smell gas or see water around live wiring, leave and call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Lift small valuables, pull rugs off wet floors, and take photos or video before you move anything. Keep children and pets out of the water.

What water damage restoration actually involves

Restoration covers far more than drying one room. Crews working to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published industry standard for water damage restoration, begin by sorting the water into categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line, category 2 is gray water with some contamination, such as a washing machine overflow, and category 3 is black water from sewage or flooding that has picked up soil and bacteria.

S500 also describes damage classes 1 to 4. They rate how much of the building is wet and how stubbornly the materials hold moisture. A class 1 loss touches a small area of low-porosity material. A class 4 loss involves dense materials such as hardwood, plaster or concrete that hold water deep inside and often need desiccant dehumidifiers.

Tools matter because water hides. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras find wet drywall, subfloor and framing that look dry. Extraction units pull standing water, air movers push dry air across surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Where materials cannot be saved, the crew removes them and applies antimicrobial treatment to what remains.

The first 24 to 72 hours, step by step

Timing shapes the outcome, because wet drywall, carpet pad and wood begin to break down and can grow mold if they stay wet. We do not promise arrival times, but we do offer same-day response when available. The sequence below is what a typical loss looks like once a crew is on site.

  1. First hour on the phone: you describe the source, the rooms affected and whether the power is on, and the dispatcher sends the nearest available crew with instructions for the wait.
  2. Arrival: the crew confirms the source is stopped, checks for electrical and slip hazards, and photographs the damage before anything is moved.
  3. Same visit: moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map the wet area, the water category is set, and you receive a written scope of work that explains the plan and the price before work starts.
  4. Next several hours: extraction units pull standing water, and carpet pad, soaked insulation and swollen drywall that cannot be saved are removed.
  5. Day one into day two: air movers and dehumidifiers go in, containment goes up where it is needed, and antimicrobial treatment is applied to affected surfaces.
  6. Days two and three: the crew returns to take moisture readings, repositions equipment and keeps drying until the readings match dry reference materials.
  7. Final visit: equipment comes out, final readings and photos are documented, and you get the record to share with your insurer.

Some losses finish drying in about three days, and others run longer. Ask the crew to show you the readings.

What drives the cost of water damage repair in Uniondale

Two homes with a wet floor can have very different bills. The biggest factor is the water category, because gray and black water call for more protective gear, more removal and more disinfecting than clean supply-line water. The affected area comes next, since more square footage means more extraction, more air movers and more drying days.

Materials change the picture too. Carpet over a wet pad can often be dried, while hardwood, plaster and insulation behave differently and may need to come out. The length of the drying period sets how long equipment stays on site, and the rebuild scope, such as new drywall, paint and flooring, is separate from the mitigation itself.

Your written scope should list each of these so you can compare it with your insurer's view of the loss. To get a real number for your property, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

How insurance usually fits into water damage repair

Policies differ, so check your policy or ask your insurer before assuming anything is or is not covered. The New York State Department of Financial Services notes that a typical homeowners or renters policy does not cover flood damage, which needs separate flood insurance. Water or sewage backup may be covered only if a water back-up endorsement was added.

File a claim as soon as possible after a loss, and document the damage before you clean anything up. Keep a list of damaged items with receipts, and hold on to damaged property until a settlement is reached. The insurer will likely send an adjuster, and you may get your own contractor's estimate to compare.

The state advises holding off on permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Emergency repairs that protect health or safety are allowed, so save your receipts. Our crews provide moisture readings and photo documentation you can hand to your adjuster, but we are not an insurer and we cannot say whether a claim will be paid.

Water damage repair in Uniondale, by cause of the loss

The right approach depends on where the water came from. Each group below covers a family of causes and points to the pages with the details. If you are unsure which fits, the full list of water damage services is sorted by symptom.

Pipes and plumbing

A burst supply pipe can release gallons a minute, and Long Island winter freezes make split lines a seasonal risk. A leaking water heater is slower but just as wet. Our crews handle the water and the drying, and a licensed plumber repairs the pipe itself.

Appliances and HVAC

Washing machine hoses, dishwashers, ice-maker lines and clogged AC condensate drains leak quietly behind panels. An appliance leak can soak cabinets and subfloor for days before a stain appears. Tell the dispatcher which appliance failed, because gray water changes how the area is cleaned.

Weather and building envelope

Roof leaks and clogged gutters send water into walls and ceilings, and a stained or sagging ceiling should be checked for trapped water before anyone pokes it. When rain arrives at ground level, flood damage restoration and basement water damage cleanup apply. We dry and clean, and a roofer closes the opening.

Water removal and drying

Standing water comes out first through water extraction, using truck-mounted and portable units. Then structural drying and dehumidification pulls moisture from framing, subfloors and the air. This is where most of the labor and equipment days sit.

Floors and walls

Hardwood cups and buckles, carpet pad holds water like a sponge, and drywall wicks moisture upward past the visible line. Whether to dry in place or replace depends on the material and how long it stayed wet. Tell the dispatcher what your flooring is, and see how hardwood floor repair differs from carpet.

Commercial properties

Shops, offices and apartment buildings along the Hempstead Turnpike corridor share walls, ceilings and plumbing stacks, so one failure can reach several tenants. Commercial water damage restoration adds after-hours access, tenant coordination and the documentation a building's insurer will ask for.

Musty smell or dark spots after a leak? Mold is our secondary service

A musty smell, fuzzy growth or dark spots on drywall after a leak usually mean moisture stayed in place too long. Drying the source comes first, and mold is a secondary service for us. The mold remediation page explains how the sequence works.

In New York, mold assessment or remediation of more than 10 square feet is licensed work, and a licensee may not do both on the same project. A licensed assessor comes first and writes the plan. A separate licensed remediator then carries it out. We dry and document the water side, and we do not perform mold testing.

ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published industry standard for mold remediation, and you can ask any remediator whether they work to it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How soon should I call after I find water damage?

Call as soon as you see it. Wet drywall, carpet pad and subfloor start to break down quickly, and the sooner the source is stopped and extraction begins, the more material can usually be saved. The line is always open, and same-day response is offered when available.

Do you handle emergency water damage at night or on weekends in Uniondale?

Yes. The 24/7 emergency line takes calls at night, on weekends and on holidays. The dispatcher asks about the source, the affected rooms and the power status, then sends the nearest available crew. We do not promise a specific arrival time, but we will tell you what to do while you wait.

What is the difference between water mitigation and water damage repair?

Water mitigation is the urgent work of stopping further damage: finding the source, extracting water, removing unsalvageable materials and drying the structure. Water damage repair is the rebuild afterward, such as new drywall, paint, flooring or cabinets. Mitigation comes first and is documented so your adjuster can see what was done.

Does insurance pay for water damage restoration?

It depends on your policy and the cause of the loss. Flood damage typically needs separate flood insurance, and sewage or water backup may be covered only with an added endorsement. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and document the damage with photos before cleaning up.

Can I dry a wet floor with fans and a dehumidifier from the hardware store?

For a small spill on a hard surface, that can work. For anything larger, consumer equipment rarely reaches moisture trapped in subfloor, wall cavities and insulation. Surface-dry material can still hold water inside, which is why crews use moisture meters and keep taking readings until the structure is dry.

What should I have ready when I call?

Know the source of the water if you can tell, when it started, which rooms are wet, whether the power is on in those areas and the name of your insurer. Photos help but are not required to call. If you are unsure of any of it, call anyway and describe what you see.
