When rain gets in during or after a storm

Storm water damage repair in Uniondale, NY starts when wind-driven rain gets through the building envelope and soaks walls, ceilings and floors. Our 24/7 line takes these calls during and after storms, so while you wait, keep clear of downed lines, move furniture and valuables away from wet walls, and catch drips with buckets.

If water is near outlets, light fixtures or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 before you call us. Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing dry.

  • Do not climb on a wet roof or ladder in wind
  • Photograph the opening and the wet areas before moving things
  • Pull wet rugs and lift curtains away from soaked walls
  • Call a roofer or siding contractor for the opening itself

Storms do not follow business hours, and after-hours storm calls get same-day response when available.

Rain that comes sideways, not up

This page is about water that gets in from outside through the building envelope: window frames, door thresholds, siding seams, flashing, vents and roof edges. It is different from rising flood water, which is handled in flood damage restoration, and from a simple roof leak above one room, covered under ceiling water damage repair.

Wind pushes rain horizontally into gaps that stay dry in ordinary showers. That is why a storm can wet a wall that has never leaked before.

Where storm rain finds a way in around Uniondale

Most houses have many seams: windows, trim, siding joints, flashing around chimneys and dormers, and door thresholds. A loose or aged seal at any of them can let wind-driven rain reach the wall cavity.

On August 18 to 19, 2024, the National Weather Service in Upton issued a flash flood warning, later upgraded to an emergency, for southeastern Nassau County and western Suffolk County, naming Levittown, with rain rates of 1 to 3 inches per hour. Uniondale was not named, and that event was flooding more than wind-driven rain. The lesson is that heavy downpours test every seal at once, so check windows and basement walls after any major storm.

What storm water damage looks like inside

  • Wet stains beneath windows, door frames or exterior corners
  • Bubbled paint or peeling wallpaper on an outside wall
  • Water running down the inside of a wall or collecting in a light fixture
  • Damp insulation, a musty smell or wet carpet along the wall
  • Swollen trim, baseboard or sill
  • Ceiling stains near the roof edge or an attic vent

How the interior gets dried and documented

Our crew works from the inside. The opening gets fixed by a roofer or siding contractor, and the crew coordinates so the interior stays dry between visits.

  1. Photograph the damage and identify the entry points with the owner.
  2. Scan walls, ceilings and floors with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera.
  3. Extract standing water and remove wet insulation, carpet pad and damaged board.
  4. Set air movers and dehumidifiers, using drying mats or cavity drying where needed.
  5. Take readings at the same points each visit, until the goal is reached.
  6. Provide written scope, readings and photos for your insurer.

Tarping is a stopgap, not a repair

A tarp or board-up keeps more rain out until the opening can be fixed. It is temporary, and it can slip in wind or hide a leak that continues beneath it.

We do not repair roofs, siding, windows or flashing. A roofer or siding contractor does, and the sooner they are booked the less likely the interior is to get wet again. Dry the building too, since a tarp over a wet wall traps the moisture.

Dry the wall in place or open it up

Dry in place

For a fresh event with firm drywall and intact insulation, cavity drying and dehumidification can save the wall. It avoids demolition and leaves less to rebuild.

Open and replace

Insulation that was soaked, soft board and anything wet for days is cut out. The cavity then dries faster and can be inspected, and new material goes in once readings are good. See wall and drywall repair for finishing.

What storm water damage costs and why

Storm losses vary a great deal. These five factors do most of the work in setting the price. If rain came through the building envelope, ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for classifying how wet the interior materials became.

  • Water category (1, 2 or 3), since rain through insulation and attics may be treated as dirty water
  • Affected area and the number of walls, ceilings and rooms involved
  • Materials involved, from drywall to plaster, flooring and insulation
  • Drying days and equipment, which depend on humidity and wall construction
  • Rebuild scope, including paint, trim and any envelope repair

Roofing or siding repair is priced by that contractor, separate from our scope. To get a real number for your property, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Storm claims and what to document

Rain that enters through a damaged building and rising flood water are treated differently, and a typical homeowners policy does not cover flood. Check your policy and ask your insurer about wind and rain damage. The New York State Department of Financial Services advises documenting losses before cleaning anything up and keeping receipts for emergency steps.

File as soon as possible, keep damaged items until a settlement is reached and hold off on permanent repairs until the adjuster has inspected. A tarp and drying are allowed protective steps, and photos and readings help the claim file.

What to look for in a storm contractor and where we work

A reliable storm damage repair contractor puts the scope in writing, explains price before work starts, is licensed and insured and shows moisture readings. Be cautious of anyone who pressures you to sign right away.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Uniondale?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7, including during and after storms. We offer same-day response when available and do not promise arrival times. Stay clear of downed lines, and call a roofer or siding contractor for the opening.

The wall under my window is wet after the storm. What now?

Move furniture away, catch drips and photograph the area. Wind-driven rain often gets past a window frame or siding seam. A moisture meter shows how far it went, and the crew dries the wall while a contractor reseals the opening.

Is this the same as flood damage?

No. Storm damage here means rain coming through the envelope. Flood means rising water from outside, which a typical homeowners policy does not cover. Both can happen in the same storm, so the source of the water matters for your claim.

Why does flat ground in Uniondale matter after a storm?

Uniondale sits on flat Long Island ground, so rain that pools against a wall or window well has nowhere quick to go. That puts steady pressure on seals and thresholds. Check low walls, window wells and door sills after any heavy downpour.

Will a tarp be enough?

A tarp is a temporary measure. It does not dry anything inside, and trapped moisture can keep damaging a wall. A roofer or siding contractor makes the permanent repair, and the interior needs drying and documentation.

Can you repair the roof or siding?

No. We dry and restore the interior. Roofing, siding, window and flashing repair belongs to those contractors, and we can coordinate timing so the building is not wetted again during drying.
