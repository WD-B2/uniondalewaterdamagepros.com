Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Uniondale, NY?

How the cause of the water shapes coverage, which losses need separate policies, and how to start a claim with the records an adjuster expects.

By the Uniondale Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ (516) 845-9582

The answer depends on how the water got in

Most homeowners policies sort water damage by cause, not by how wet the floor looks. A supply line that splits overnight is treated differently from a drip that ran behind a wall for months. Your declarations page and the full policy wording decide the answer, and only your insurer can say whether a specific loss is covered.

This guide covers the general patterns and what the New York State Department of Financial Services (DFS) tells consumers. It is not a coverage opinion, and nothing here promises that any claim will be paid.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual or neglected

Policies commonly separate sudden and accidental discharge from damage that builds up slowly. A washing machine hose that bursts is the sudden kind. A slow leak under a sink that went unnoticed, or a pipe that corroded over time, is the gradual kind, and many policies exclude it or limit it.

Maintenance matters too. Insurers often point to wear, rust, or an ignored repair as a sign that a loss could have been prevented. Wording varies from policy to policy, so the exclusions list is worth reading before you need it.

Flood and water backup are separate questions

DFS states that the typical homeowners or renter's policy does not cover flood damage. Flood needs its own policy, and the DFS guidance points to the National Flood Insurance Program as one source. If surface water entered after a downpour, the drying side of that loss is described on the flood damage restoration page.

Water back-up and sewage damage may be covered only if a water back-up endorsement, also called a rider, was added to your policy. DFS adds that policies typically exclude backup caused by flood. If a drain overflowed inside the house, the page on sewage backup cleanup explains why that water is handled as a contamination problem.

What to check in your policy today

Ten minutes with the paperwork now saves guessing later. Look for these items and write down the page numbers.

  • The declarations page, for your deductible and any water or sewer endorsement
  • The exclusions list, for words like seepage, continuous, repeated, wear, and maintenance
  • Whether a water back-up endorsement exists and what limit it carries
  • Whether a separate flood policy is in force
  • Any duty to protect the property from further damage, and any rule about approving repairs
  • If you rent, what your renter's policy says about belongings and who insures the building

If a term is unclear, call your agent or insurer and ask in plain words. Write down the date, the name of the person, and what they said.

How to start a claim without weakening it

The DFS consumer guidance gives a clear order of operations. These steps follow it.

  1. File as soon as possible after the loss, with the insurer that sold the policy, following the instructions in the policy.
  2. Photograph the damage before you start cleaning. Take photos or video of the damage and of the water source.
  3. Make only emergency repairs needed to protect health, safety, or the property from more damage, and save every receipt.
  4. Keep damaged property until a settlement is reached, and list each item with receipts or proof of value.
  5. Keep a record of every conversation with your insurer, including dates and names.
  6. Expect an adjuster. DFS says permanent repairs should not be made until the insurer has inspected the loss.

DFS advises filing if you have a good-faith basis to believe the policy covers the loss. It gives no set number of days for filing or for an insurer to respond, so check your own policy for notice terms and do not wait.

Records that help the adjuster

A restoration crew can supply moisture readings, photos, and a written scope of work that you can hand to your adjuster. Those records show what was wet, what was removed, and how drying was tracked. They support your claim file, but the insurer makes the coverage decision.

Insurance questions that come up in Uniondale, NY

Uniondale sits on flat Long Island ground about 52 feet above sea level, so water from a heavy downpour can pool instead of draining away quickly. The New York State Department of Financial Services consumer guidance says a typical homeowners or renter's policy does not cover flood damage. A homeowner here should read the flood and water back-up lines of the policy before storm season, not after a loss.

Because Uniondale is an unincorporated hamlet in the Town of Hempstead, permit questions for repairs go to the Town of Hempstead Building Department. Settle the insurer's inspection first, then ask about permits before any rebuild is scheduled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies treat a sudden pipe break as a covered cause of loss, but your policy decides, not a general rule. The damaged pipe itself and the water damage it caused may be handled differently, and your deductible applies. File promptly and ask your insurer what applies to your policy.

What if the water came from a slow leak?

Gradual or repeated leakage is a common exclusion, so expect questions about when the leak started. Photograph the damage, note the date you found it, and keep any plumber's report. You can also get your own contractor's estimate to compare with the insurer's.

Can drying start before the adjuster visits?

DFS allows emergency repairs to protect health and safety and says to save receipts. Permanent repairs should wait until the insurer has inspected the loss. Take photos first, then ask your insurer what it wants documented before water removal begins.

How do I know if I have a water back-up endorsement?

Check the declarations page and the list of endorsements attached to the policy. If you do not see one, call your agent and ask directly. DFS notes that backup and sewage damage may be covered only when such an endorsement was added.

Who do I ask about permits for repairs in Uniondale?

Uniondale is unincorporated, so the Town of Hempstead Building Department is the place to ask what permits a repair needs. A licensed electrician or plumber pulls those permits where required. Ask before you schedule rebuilding, not after.
