Do Water Damage Repairs Need a Permit in Uniondale, NY?

The honest answer starts with a question to the right office. Here is who to ask and how to prepare.

By the Uniondale Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ (516) 845-9582

The honest answer starts with asking

Nobody can tell you from a blog post if your particular repair needs a permit. The answer depends on the work, the building and the rules the local building department enforces. What we can do is point you to the right office and help you ask a useful question.

In Uniondale, NY that office is the Town of Hempstead Building Department. Uniondale is an unincorporated hamlet in the Town of Hempstead, Nassau County, so there is no separate village hall to call.

What the Town of Hempstead says about its Building Department

The Town of Hempstead's Building Department page says the department aims to preserve life and property and that it administers and enforces building, plumbing, electrical and housing codes. It points people to its Online Permit Center for building permits and forms, and lists an email address and phone lines for building and plumbing inspectors.

The page also says Plans Examiner appointments require a call one day ahead, and it lists front counter hours. Check the page itself for current contacts, since hours and numbers change. We do not decide what needs a permit, and neither does a restoration crew.

How to ask a useful question

A vague question gets a vague answer. Describe the work you plan to do, not just the cause. Instead of asking whether water damage needs a permit, say what will be taken out and put back.

Have your address, your tax map or parcel information if you know it, and a short written description ready. Ask whether the work needs a permit, who is allowed to apply, and whether an inspection is required before walls are closed. Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and what they told you.

  1. List the work in plain words: which rooms, which materials come out, which go back in.
  2. Note whether it touches electrical wiring, plumbing, gas or structure.
  3. Check the Town of Hempstead Building Department page and its Online Permit Center.
  4. Contact the department by email or phone and ask your question using your list.
  5. Save the answer in writing or in your own dated notes.

Licensed trades and who pulls permits

Electrical and plumbing work is for licensed tradespeople. If wiring was in a wet wall or a pipe has to be replaced, a licensed electrician or plumber handles it, and where a permit is required, that tradesperson pulls it. The Town of Hempstead page links to electrician and plumber licensing information separately.

We do not repair wiring or plumbing, and a restoration crew does not pull permits for those trades. If a scope includes a rebuild phase, such as wall and drywall repair or ceiling repair, ask the contractor who is responsible for permit questions on the lines that need one.

Why it matters to insurers and to resale

The New York State Department of Financial Services says permanent repairs should not be made until insurers have inspected the loss. It also says emergency repairs that prevent further damage are different, and that you should save receipts. Permit questions fit in the same timeline: ask early, document the answer, and keep the paperwork with your claim file. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it expects.

Records also matter later. A future buyer, lender or inspector may ask what was repaired and how. A file that holds photos, drying logs, contractor scopes and any permit or inspection paperwork answers those questions without guesswork.

Where drying and permits meet

Drying and emergency steps usually come first, and permits are a repair-phase question. Crews dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers while tracking readings with moisture meters, and they document it with photos you can give your adjuster.

We dispatch to homes and businesses across Uniondale and nearby areas, and our written scope separates emergency work from rebuild so you can ask the permit question at the right moment.

Uniondale is unincorporated: who to ask

Uniondale has no village government of its own. The Town of Hempstead's official Building Department page is the place to start, and it lists an Online Permit Center, an email for questions and front counter hours. Check it before you travel or call, because details can change.

The practical consequence for a Uniondale homeowner is that your permit question goes to the town, because there is no village clerk to ask. Ask the town, write down the answer, and share it with your contractor and your insurer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does drywall replacement after a leak need a permit?

We cannot say. The answer depends on the work and on what the Town of Hempstead Building Department requires. Describe exactly what will be removed and replaced, and ask the department directly through its page or Online Permit Center.

Who pulls the permit, me or the contractor?

It depends on the work and on the department's requirements. Where a permit is required for electrical or plumbing work, a licensed electrician or plumber pulls it. Ask each contractor in writing who is responsible, and confirm with the Building Department.

Can I start repairs before I hear back from the town?

Emergency steps to stop further damage are different from permanent repairs. Drying and protecting the property can start right away, but ask before you cover up or replace anything. Your insurer may also want to inspect before permanent work, so ask there as well.

Will my insurer care about permits?

Possibly. Check your policy and ask your insurer what documentation it wants. Keep photos, scopes, receipts and any permit or inspection paperwork together, because a complete file is easier to explain.

Which office handles permit questions for a house in Uniondale?

Uniondale is unincorporated, so the Town of Hempstead Building Department is where to ask. Its page lists an Online Permit Center, an email address and phone lines. Check the page for current contacts and hours.
