Black Mold in the Shower: A Uniondale, NY Homeowner Guide

What the dark line along your caulk usually is, what a homeowner can clean, and the signs that mean it is time to stop.

By the Uniondale Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ (516) 845-9582

A dark line along the caulk

Most people notice it first at the bottom corner of the tub or along the grout. A gray or black line shows up, wipes away a little, and returns within weeks. Color alone cannot tell you what it is, so the useful question is why that spot stays wet.

In a shower, the usual ingredients are moisture, soap scum, body oils, and caulk or grout that stays damp. Those together feed dark growth on the surface. The same patch can also be old staining that no longer wipes off.

What it usually is, and what it can hide

On tile, glass, and a sealed tub, the dark film is typically a surface problem. Caulk is different. It is porous at the edges once it cracks or peels, and growth can sit behind it where a cloth cannot reach.

That is why a line that returns quickly after cleaning points to the caulk, a tiny gap, or water getting behind the surface. The surface is the symptom. The moisture is the cause.

Small-area cleaning versus when to stop

The New York State Department of Labor household guidance says a homeowner may handle areas of about 10 square feet or less if no one in the home has a mold sensitivity. For routine cleaning, the guidance points to non-ammonia soap and hot water. It warns never to mix ammonia and bleach, and it does not recommend biocides as a routine cleanup.

Use gloves, open a window or run the fan, and dry the area when you finish. If you cannot tell whether someone in the home is sensitive, do not take the chance.

Ventilation and caulk habits that help

The Department of Labor says the key to mold control is moisture control, including ventilation, humidity control, and fixing leaks. In a bathroom, a few habits do most of the work.

  • Run the exhaust fan during the shower and for a while afterward
  • Wipe or squeegee glass and tile so water does not sit in the corners
  • Leave the shower curtain open so it dries instead of bunching damp
  • Wash soap scum off before it builds up on the lower tiles
  • Replace caulk once it cracks, peels, or stays discolored after cleaning
  • Fix drips at the showerhead or valve so the wall does not stay wet

These habits reduce the moisture mold needs, but no habit can promise growth will not return. A bathroom that stays damp for other reasons will keep showing it.

A short cleaning routine for small areas

If the patch is small and the house has no one sensitive to mold, a plain routine is enough. Wear gloves and eye protection, open a window or run the fan, and keep pets and children out of the room while you work.

Scrub with non-ammonia soap and hot water, rinse, and dry the surface with a towel. Then leave the door and fan on so the area dries fully. If you replace caulk, remove the old bead completely, let the joint dry, and apply a fresh bead.

  • Do not mix ammonia with bleach, as the Department of Labor warns
  • Do not scrub soft drywall or ceiling board, since that can spread material
  • Stop and reassess if the stain grows back within days

When the wall behind the shower is the problem

A soft spot, a bulge, or a stain on the wall behind the shower suggests water is getting inside. Cleaning the tile will not help that. The wall needs to be opened and dried, and damaged board replaced, which is covered on the page about wall and drywall water damage repair.

If the growth is larger than about 10 square feet, New York treats it as a mold project that needs licensed professionals. Our page on mold remediation explains the order: a licensed assessor first, then a separate licensed remediator. We do not repair the shower valve or pipes, which is a licensed plumber's job.

Shower moisture in Uniondale, NY homes

Long Island has humid summers and four distinct seasons, and a bathroom handles both. In humid weather, the air may hold so much moisture that a shower surface stays damp long after you step out. The New York State Department of Labor household guidance puts moisture control at the center of mold prevention, so the practical tie-back is a fan that runs long enough to clear the room.

Uniondale is unincorporated and sits within the Town of Hempstead. If a bathroom fan needs to be added or vented differently, ask the Town of Hempstead Building Department about permits, and a licensed electrician pulls electrical permits where required. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the black stuff in my shower always mold?

Not always. Dark staining can be soap scum, mildew, old stains, or dirt trapped in caulk. You cannot be sure by looking at it. If it keeps returning after cleaning, treat the cause as moisture and consider the caulk.

Can I use bleach on it?

The Department of Labor household guidance recommends non-ammonia soap and hot water, warns never to mix ammonia and bleach, and does not recommend biocides as a routine cleanup. Keep the room ventilated and follow the label of any cleaner you use.

How often should I replace shower caulk?

There is no fixed interval. Replace it when it cracks, peels, pulls away, or stays dark after cleaning. Let the area dry fully before applying new caulk, or you seal moisture inside.

Why does it keep coming back?

Because the spot stays wet or the caulk has failed. Cleaning removes what you can see, but it does not change the moisture. No cleaning or habit can promise it will not return.

Do humid Uniondale summers make shower mold worse?

Humid weather leaves more moisture in the air, and a bathroom without a working exhaust fan dries slowly. Long Island summers are humid, so run the fan longer then. Ask the Town of Hempstead Building Department before changing how a fan is vented.
