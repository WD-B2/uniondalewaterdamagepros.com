Denied Water Damage Claim in New York: A Uniondale, NY Guide

What to do when a denial letter or a low payment arrives, based on New York State Department of Financial Services guidance.

By the Uniondale Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ (516) 845-9582

The denial letter is the start of a paper trail

A denial or a low payment feels final, but it is the point where good records matter most. Read the letter slowly, note the date it arrived, and keep the envelope or email. Everything you do next should be written down.

This guide is general information based on New York State Department of Financial Services (DFS) consumer guidance. It is not legal advice, and it cannot promise any outcome.

Ask for the reason in writing, tied to the policy

DFS says that when a claim is denied, the insurer should provide a reason that is supported by the language in the policy. If the letter is vague, ask for the specific policy provision it relies on. Put the request in writing and keep a copy.

Then read that provision yourself, next to your declarations page. Compare what the insurer says happened with what you documented, including the cause of the water and when you found it.

Compare your own contractor's estimate

DFS notes you may get your own estimate from a contractor to compare with the insurance company's estimate. A fair comparison looks at the same scope: water removal, materials taken out, drying equipment and days, monitoring, and rebuilding.

Underpayments often come from missing line items. A written scope that lists water extraction work, drying equipment, and moisture readings makes gaps easier to see. The estimate does not prove the insurer wrong, but it gives you a specific point to raise.

What to do next, in order

Work through these steps and keep a copy of every document you send.

  1. Request the denial reason in writing and the policy language it relies on.
  2. Gather your photos, video, inventory, receipts, and any plumber or contractor reports.
  3. Get a contractor's written estimate for the same scope and compare it line by line.
  4. Send your insurer a short written reply with the documents that address its stated reason.
  5. Keep damaged property until a settlement is reached, as DFS advises.
  6. If you cannot resolve it, ask DFS for help through its consumer help channels.

Emergency repairs and receipts

DFS allows emergency repairs needed to protect health, safety, or the property from more damage, and tells policyholders to save every receipt. It also says permanent repairs should not be made until the insurer has inspected the loss. If a dispute is open, that line matters.

Drying equipment, extraction, and tear-out of wet materials are usually emergency-side work, and the structure can be damaged further while a dispute drags on. A crew can document readings and supply structural drying and dehumidification records, so the condition of the building is on paper.

Where DFS can help

The DFS disaster and flooding consumer page lists a consumer hotline and a way to file a complaint online. The agency says to contact it if you cannot reach your insurer or you disagree with a denial. Check the DFS website for current contact details before you call.

A complaint may not change the decision. It does put your documents in front of a state regulator, so organize them first. Put the claim number, the dates, the denial letter, and a one-page timeline on top, with photos and estimates behind them. A short, orderly packet is easier for anyone to review than a pile of emails.

Keeping records that hold up

Good records are the one part of a dispute you fully control. DFS tells policyholders to document losses before cleaning anything up, to keep a detailed inventory with receipts or proof of value, and to keep a record of every conversation with the insurer.

Add a simple log of your own. Each entry gets a date, the person you spoke with, and what was said or promised. If a phone call changes anything, follow up with an email that restates it, so the record exists in writing.

  • Photos and video taken before any cleanup
  • The inventory of damaged items with receipts or proof of value
  • Every estimate, invoice, and emergency repair receipt
  • Moisture readings and drying logs from the crew
  • The denial letter, policy pages, and your written replies

Claim disputes and repairs in Uniondale, NY

Uniondale is an unincorporated hamlet in the Town of Hempstead, so there is no village hall to handle a dispute. The New York State Department of Financial Services is the state-level place to ask for help with an insurer, and the Town of Hempstead Building Department is where to ask about permits for repairs. Keep those two tracks separate in your notes.

If a rebuild needs a licensed electrician or plumber, they pull the permits where required. Confirm with the town before you schedule that work, and wait for the insurer's inspection first. Crews cover the area on the service area page.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as a valid reason for denial?

DFS says the reason should be supported by the policy language. A reason should point to a specific provision, not just a general statement. If it does not, ask for the provision in writing and compare it with what you documented.

Can I make repairs while the dispute is open?

Emergency repairs to protect health, safety, or the property are allowed under DFS guidance, and you should save receipts. Permanent repairs should wait until the insurer has inspected the loss. Tell your insurer in writing what you plan to do and keep the reply.

Should I throw away damaged items?

DFS says damaged personal property should be kept until an insurance settlement is reached. Photograph everything before cleaning and list each item with proof of value. If an item is a health hazard, photograph it first and ask your insurer how to proceed.

Does a contractor's estimate prove my insurer is wrong?

No. It gives you a comparison point and a detailed scope to discuss. The insurer still decides coverage under the policy, and a difference in numbers may reflect a different scope or a different reading of the policy.

Is there a Uniondale office I can go to about a denial?

No. Claim disputes go through your insurer and the state agency, DFS, which accepts complaints online and by phone. The Town of Hempstead is relevant only for permits on repairs, not for the claim itself.
