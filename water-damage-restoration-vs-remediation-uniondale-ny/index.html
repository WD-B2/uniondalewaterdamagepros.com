Water Damage Restoration vs Remediation in Uniondale, NY

Two words that look alike on a quote and mean different work. Here is how to tell them apart before you sign.

By the Uniondale Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ (516) 845-9582

One quote, two words that look the same

Pick up a written quote after a leak and you may see restoration on one line and remediation on another. Some contractors use the words as if they were the same. Others use them for separate phases, and in one case New York law gives a word a legal meaning.

This guide is for homeowners and landlords in Uniondale, NY who are comparing paperwork. It explains how the words are commonly used, where the state draws a hard line, and what to ask when a scope of work leaves the meaning unclear.

What restoration usually means

In everyday use, restoration is the larger idea: getting the property back to its pre-loss condition. It often covers the emergency phase (stopping the source, extracting water, drying), then the repair phase (replacing drywall, flooring, trim and paint).

Industry standards such as ANSI/IICRC S500 describe the drying side of this work, including water categories 1, 2 and 3 and damage classes 1 to 4. The standard is a published guide for the trade, not a certification we hold. A good scope tells you which phase each line belongs to.

What remediation usually means

Remediation is the narrower word. It usually means correcting a specific problem, such as removing contaminated materials or cleaning a space so a hazard is gone. On a water loss, you may see it attached to drying, to cleanup of contaminated water, or to mold.

Because the word is flexible, never assume. Ask what is being remediated, how the work will be measured, and what finished looks like. Read a line that says only remediation as a question, not an answer.

Why New York separates assessment from remediation for mold

When the problem is mold, remediation is not loose language. Under Article 32 of the New York Labor Law, the state Department of Labor licenses mold assessors, remediation contractors and abatement workers. The Department of Labor says the same individual may not perform both the assessment and the remediation on the same property.

The assessor inspects, identifies the moisture source and writes a remediation plan that includes an estimate of cost and time. The contractor then does the physical work described in the plan, and the assessor checks the result afterward. The state does not require owners to clean up mold, but for an area larger than 10 square feet it says to use a licensed mold professional.

Owners working on their own property are a limited exception. For you as a homeowner, the practical point is that a quote that bundles mold testing and mold removal from one company deserves questions. See how mold remediation is handled after a water loss for the sequence.

A mitigation-only scope is a legitimate first step, because drying should not wait for repair decisions. Just make sure the paperwork says clearly that rebuild work is a later, separate quote.

Why drying comes before either word

Whether a quote says restoration or remediation, the water has to be dealt with first. Crews map moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera, then run air movers and dehumidifiers until readings return to a normal range. That work is described under structural drying and dehumidification.

We do not perform mold assessment. When a scope calls for it, a separate licensed assessor writes the plan, and a separate remediator carries it out.

Questions to ask before you sign

Ask each contractor to define restoration and remediation in the scope. Ask which line items are mitigation and which are rebuild, and whether any mold-related work is included. The New York State Department of Financial Services says you may get an estimate from your own contractor to compare with the insurance company's estimate.

Check your policy and ask your insurer what it expects from the paperwork. Photograph the damage and keep your receipts, and do not make permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected the loss.

  • Ask for the terms to be defined in plain words in the written scope.
  • Ask who verifies a finished job, and how.
  • Ask for license numbers for any mold-related work and check them with the Department of Labor's online search.
  • Ask what happens if mold is found after demolition.

Uniondale: state mold rules and your local lookup

Uniondale is an unincorporated hamlet in the Town of Hempstead, Nassau County, and the mold licensing rules come from New York State, not the town. The New York State Department of Labor publishes the rules and offers an online search where you can verify an assessor or remediator before you hire one.

That makes the check simple for a Uniondale homeowner. Ask for the license, search it yourself, and confirm that the assessor and the remediator are different companies. Permit questions about repair work are a separate matter for the Town of Hempstead Building Department.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is restoration the same as remediation?

Not always. Restoration usually describes getting the property back to its earlier condition, from drying through repair. Remediation is a narrower word for correcting a problem such as contamination or mold. Contractors use both loosely, so ask for each to be defined in the written scope.

Can one company assess and remove my mold?

Not on the same project in New York. The Department of Labor says the same individual may not perform both the assessment and the remediation. A separate licensed assessor defines the scope, and the remediator carries out that plan.

Do I need a licensed professional for a small patch of mold?

The Department of Labor says owners are not required to clean up mold, and that for an area larger than 10 square feet you must use a licensed mold professional. Owners working on their own property are a limited exception. If you are unsure of the size or have a sensitivity, ask a licensed assessor.

Does a water damage quote that says remediation mean mold work?

Not necessarily. On a water loss the word may refer to drying, cleanup of contaminated water or mold. Ask exactly what is being removed or treated, and whether the line is part of a licensed mold project.

Do the same mold rules apply in Uniondale as elsewhere in New York?

Yes. Labor Law Article 32 is a statewide law, so it applies to Uniondale and the rest of Nassau County. Ask any mold contractor or assessor for license details and verify them yourself through the Department of Labor's online search tool.
