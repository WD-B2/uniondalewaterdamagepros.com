Selling a House After Water Damage in Uniondale, NY

Disclosure basics, the records that help, and the questions to take to your attorney and agent.

By the Uniondale Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ (516) 845-9582

The leak is fixed, and now a buyer is asking

You dried the room, repaired the wall and painted over the stain. Months later you list the house, and a buyer's agent asks whether there has ever been water in it. The question is simple, but the answer touches paperwork, records and law.

This guide is for Uniondale, NY homeowners thinking about selling a home that had water damage. It covers the New York disclosure form in general terms, the records worth keeping and the questions for your attorney. It is not legal advice, and it does not tell you what to put on any form.

What the New York Department of State says about the disclosure statement

The New York State Department of State publishes the Property Condition Disclosure Statement form. Its page says the seller of residential real property must make sure the statement, or a copy, is delivered to the buyer or the buyer's agent before the buyer signs a binding contract of sale.

The seller completes the form from actual knowledge, and may mark NA for items that do not apply and Unkn for items the seller does not know. The page says the form applies to one- to four-family homes used or intended as a residence, and it excludes unimproved land, condominium units and cooperative apartments.

What the form page says about consequences

The same page says that if the statement is not delivered before the buyer signs the contract, the buyer receives a credit against the agreed purchase price when title transfers.

The page also says a knowingly false or incomplete statement may subject the seller to claims by the buyer. If the seller later learns something that makes a delivered statement materially inaccurate, the page says the seller should deliver a revised one as soon as practicable. It describes the form as not a warranty and not a substitute for inspections or tests.

Decision framework: what to do with each kind of past water event

The framework below is for organizing your thinking before you meet your attorney and agent. It does not decide what the form requires. Read each question on the form itself, and ask your attorney how to answer it for your situation.

Keep a loss file that a buyer can read

The New York State Department of Financial Services advises taking photos or video of damage before cleaning anything up, and saving receipts for emergency repairs. Those same records become the backbone of a seller's file. Start it while the loss is fresh, because details fade and paper gets lost.

A useful file has five parts: dated photos, the insurer or claim correspondence if there was a claim, the contractor's written scope and invoices, moisture readings and drying logs, and any inspection or permit paperwork. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it expects, since your claim file belongs to the loss, not to the sale.

Why drying records carry weight

A buyer's inspector will want to know whether wet materials were dried or only covered up. Crews who handle structural drying and dehumidification log moisture meter readings during the job, and those logs show the structure moving toward normal. A photo of a painted wall tells a buyer little. A set of dated readings tells them much more.

If mold was part of the story, the paperwork has an extra layer. New York separates mold assessment from remediation, so a project may have an assessor's plan and a separate remediator's work. Having both on hand answers a buyer's questions, and mold remediation after water damage explains the sequence.

Before you list: a short to-do list

A few quiet steps before the listing go a long way. None of them replaces advice from a lawyer.

  • Gather the loss file into one folder, printed or digital.
  • Read the Department of State form before you list, not on the day of a buyer's request.
  • Ask your attorney and agent how to describe past water events.
  • Ask contractors for written copies of anything they documented.
  • Do not repair or repaint in a way that hides a problem you have not resolved.
  • Keep every receipt and written note until after closing.

Uniondale sellers: a regional reason to keep records

Long Island has humid summers and winter freezes, so buyers and inspectors have reason to ask about moisture in any house. A Uniondale seller who can answer with dated records is in a better position than one who relies on memory.

The New York State Department of State disclosure page applies to residential sales across the state, including one- to four-family homes in Uniondale. Ask your attorney and agent how it applies to your sale, and keep your loss file ready for them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to disclose past water damage when I sell in New York?

We cannot answer that for your sale. The Department of State page says the seller of residential real property must deliver the Property Condition Disclosure Statement to the buyer before the contract is signed, and the form asks the seller to answer from actual knowledge. Ask your attorney how it applies to your situation.

What happens if I do not give the buyer the form?

According to the Department of State page, if the statement is not delivered before the buyer signs a binding contract, the buyer receives a credit against the purchase price when title transfers. Ask your attorney how that works in a real transaction.

What if I do not know whether a previous owner had a leak?

The page says the seller may mark Unkn for items the seller does not know, and that the form is completed from actual knowledge. Ask your attorney how to handle that in your case, and share any inspection reports you have.

Will repair records really matter to a buyer?

They often help. Photos, drying logs and invoices answer questions an inspector or buyer may raise, and they show the work was documented. They cannot promise a sale or a price, but they replace guesswork with paper.

Where should a Uniondale seller start?

Start with your attorney and your agent, and have your loss file ready. In Uniondale, NY the Department of State disclosure form applies to one- to four-family homes, so read it early. Take the form and your records to the first meeting.
